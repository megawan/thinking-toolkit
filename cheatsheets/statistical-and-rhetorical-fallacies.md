# Statistical and Rhetorical Fallacies: cheat sheet

> Reasoning errors that misuse numbers, win by tricks, or break logical form.

Full guide: [Statistical and Rhetorical Fallacies](../methods/critical-thinking/statistical-and-rhetorical-fallacies.md)

## Use it when

- Checking a claim that rests on statistics or probability
- A persuasive argument that avoids answering the question
- Reviewing your own report before you quote numbers

## Steps

1. Identify the type of claim: numbers, persuasion or logic
2. For numbers: compared with what, how many cases, how chosen, predicted or found after looking?
3. For chance: are the events really independent?
4. For tricks: restate the move in plain words
5. For logic: write the form (If P then Q...) and compare with valid forms
6. Ask for the missing comparison, sample, criterion or reason
7. Keep the conclusion open
8. Check your own numbers and arguments

## Key questions

- What is the base rate?
- Compared with what?
- Was the pattern predicted, or found after looking?
- What did we agree the standard was?
- Is this form valid?

## Watch out for

- Naming fallacies instead of explaining them
- Rejecting a conclusion because of a flaw
- Applying statistical rules without context
