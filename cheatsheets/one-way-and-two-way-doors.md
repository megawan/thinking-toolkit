# One-way and Two-way Doors: cheat sheet

> Sort decisions by how hard they are to undo, then decide fast or carefully.

Full guide: [One-way and Two-way Doors](../methods/decision-making/one-way-and-two-way-doors.md)

## Use it when

- Meetings drag on over minor decisions
- You are about to make a big commitment
- You need a rule for who decides what

## Steps

1. Name the decision in a sentence
2. Ask what it would take to undo it (time, money, reputation)
3. Classify: two-way (easy to reverse), one-way (hard), or in between
4. Two-way: decide now with the person closest, set a review date
5. One-way: gather data, consult, use a method such as Decision Matrix or Pre-mortem
6. Try to make one-way decisions reversible (pilot, rent, short contract)
7. Record the decision and the signal that would reverse it

## Key questions

- Can we undo this? At what cost?
- What is the worst case if we are wrong?
- Can we test a smaller version first?
- What early signal tells us we were wrong?

## Watch out for

- Treating every decision as one-way
- Treating one-way decisions as two-way
- Never reviewing a two-way decision
