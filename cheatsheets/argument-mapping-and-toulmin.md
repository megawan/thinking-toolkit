# Argument Mapping and Toulmin Model: cheat sheet

> Lay out an argument as claim, evidence and the link between them.

Full guide: [Argument Mapping and Toulmin Model](../methods/critical-thinking/argument-mapping-and-toulmin.md)

## Use it when

- Reviewing a proposal or business case
- Preparing an essay, pitch or policy brief
- Locating where two sides really differ

## Steps

1. State the claim in one sentence
2. List the grounds (facts, evidence) and their sources
3. Write out the warrant: why the grounds support the claim
4. Add backing for the warrant
5. Add a qualifier (probably, usually)
6. List rebuttals: when it would not hold
7. Test each link
8. Decide: accept, weaken, ask for more, or reject

## Key questions

- What are you asking me to accept?
- What is that based on?
- Why do these grounds support this claim?
- When would it not hold?

## Watch out for

- A missing warrant
- Mapping only your own side
- Treating the model as a verdict
