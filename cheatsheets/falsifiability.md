# Falsifiability: cheat sheet

> Ask what result would prove you wrong.

Full guide: [Falsifiability](../methods/critical-thinking/falsifiability.md)

## Use it when

- Setting up a test, pilot or experiment
- Judging a forecast or strategy that always seems right
- Reviewing a claim that comes with many excuses

## Steps

1. State the hypothesis clearly
2. Ask: what observation would count against it?
3. Add numbers, time frame and conditions
4. Make the prediction risky
5. Agree success and failure criteria before the test
6. Run it and record the result
7. If it fails, revise or drop it; do not rescue it with an excuse
8. Check the background assumptions of the test

## Key questions

- What would change my mind?
- How much, by when, for whom?
- Could this prediction easily turn out wrong?
- Did we agree the criteria before we saw the result?

## Watch out for

- Unfalsifiable wording ('might', 'eventually')
- Moving the goalposts
- Ad hoc rescue of a failing idea
