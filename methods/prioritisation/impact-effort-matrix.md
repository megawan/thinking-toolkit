# Impact-Effort Matrix

> Plot ideas by how much they would achieve and how much they would cost, to spot quick wins and avoid money pits.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Prioritisation |
| **Time needed** | 20-45 minutes with a group |
| **Best for** | Quickly sorting a list of ideas or tasks when you need a shared picture, not a precise score |

## What it is

The impact-effort matrix, sometimes called the action priority matrix, is a 2x2 grid. The vertical axis is the impact (value) an item would bring, and the horizontal axis is the effort (cost, time, difficulty) it requires. Each item is placed on the grid, giving four groups.

| | Low effort | High effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High impact** | **Quick wins:** do first | **Major projects:** plan carefully; do after quick wins |
| **Low impact** | **Fill-ins:** do if time allows | **Money pits:** avoid or drop |

## Why it works

It reduces a complicated trade-off to a picture the whole team can see and move items around. It exposes the two extremes: easy things that deliver value are done first, and expensive things that deliver little are dropped. Because it is quick, it is useful where a formal scoring model is too heavy.

## When to use

- A group needs a shared view of a backlog
- You want to find quick wins to build momentum
- An initial rough sort before a deeper method
- Workshops, retrospectives and planning sessions

## When not to use

- You need a precise ranking between close items; use [RICE and ICE Scoring](rice-and-ice-scoring.md)
- Impact is hard to compare across very different items
- Some items are mandatory regardless of their position; sort those separately

## How to apply

1. **Define the goal.** Impact is only meaningful relative to something, such as revenue, customer satisfaction or risk reduction.
2. **List the items,** one per sticky note.
3. **Define the axes.** Agree what high and low mean for impact and effort. For effort, include time, money and complexity.
4. **Place each item.** Do it together, or each person places notes and the group discusses the differences.
5. **Discuss outliers.** Items that people place far apart reveal different assumptions.
6. **Identify the four groups.**
7. **Plan the order.** Quick wins first, then major projects chosen for their value, fill-ins when there is slack, and money pits dropped or reconsidered.
8. **Look for ways to move items.** Can a major project be split into a quick win? Can effort be reduced with a simpler version?
9. **Record the result** and review it at the next planning session.

## Example: improving an online shop

| Idea | Impact | Effort | Quadrant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Show delivery cost earlier in checkout | High | Low | Quick win |
| Add guest checkout | High | Medium | Quick win or major project, depending on the platform |
| Rebuild the whole site | High | High | Major project |
| Redesign the footer | Low | Low | Fill-in |
| Add a loyalty programme | Medium | High | Major project, test with a small pilot |
| Custom animation on home page | Low | High | Money pit |

**Plan:** Do the delivery cost change and guest checkout first. Run a small pilot of the loyalty programme to learn before building it fully. Drop the animation. Use the footer redesign as a filler task.

## Tips

- Use relative placement; the grid does not need exact scores
- Add a third dimension with colour, for example risk or confidence
- Treat the middle with care; items near the centre may need more information
- Revisit the matrix after finishing the quick wins, since the picture changes

## Pitfalls

- **Vague impact.** Without a goal, everyone places notes by feeling.
- **Underestimating effort.** Include testing, support and maintenance.
- **Optimism in high impact.** Back it with evidence.
- **Ignoring dependencies.** A low-impact item may unlock a high-impact one.
- **Doing only quick wins.** The big wins are often major projects.
- **Treating the chart as final.** It is a conversation starter.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Goal for impact defined
- [ ] Effort includes time, cost and complexity
- [ ] Items placed and outliers discussed
- [ ] Quick wins identified and scheduled
- [ ] Major projects chosen deliberately
- [ ] Money pits dropped or reframed
