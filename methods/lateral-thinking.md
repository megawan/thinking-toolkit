# Lateral Thinking

**Category:** Idea generation

## What it is

A set of techniques, popularised by Edward de Bono, for approaching a problem from unexpected angles instead of following the most obvious logical path. The goal is to break fixed patterns of thought, not to replace logical analysis.

## When to use

- You are stuck and every option looks the same
- Conventional solutions have already failed
- You need many ideas before choosing one

## Techniques

### Challenge assumptions
List what you take for granted about the problem, then ask what happens if each one is false.

### Random input
Pick an unrelated word or object and force a connection to your problem. The connection does not need to make sense at first; look for what it suggests.

### Provocation (PO)
Make a deliberately absurd statement ("PO: the customer pays us after we deliver nothing") and ask what useful idea it could lead to.

### Reversal
Flip the problem. Instead of "how do we get more customers?", ask "how would we lose every customer?" and invert the answers.

### Alternatives
Do not stop at the first workable idea. Set a target (for example, ten different approaches) before evaluating any of them.

## How to apply

1. State the problem in one sentence.
2. Pick one technique and generate ideas without judging them.
3. Repeat with a second technique.
4. Only then evaluate: keep ideas that are feasible, or that hint at something feasible.

## Example

**Problem:** Customers often leave a checkout page without paying.

- *Reversal:* How would we make people abandon checkout on purpose? Ask for account creation, hide shipping costs, add many fields. Remove each of these.
- *Random input* ("umbrella"): protection, covers you from something. Show a guarantee or free returns at the payment step.

## Pitfalls

- Judging ideas too early
- Treating a wild idea as the answer instead of a stepping stone
- Skipping the evaluation step
