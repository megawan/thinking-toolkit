# Crazy 8s

> Fold a sheet into eight panels and sketch eight different ideas in eight minutes, one per minute.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Idea generation |
| **Time needed** | 8 minutes of sketching, plus 15-30 minutes to share and choose |
| **Best for** | Quickly pushing past the first obvious idea, especially for designs, features, and layouts |

## What it is

Crazy 8s is a fast sketching exercise. You fold a sheet of paper into eight panels and draw a different idea in each panel, spending one minute on each. It became widely known through the design sprint process used at Google Ventures.

The tight time limit is the point. One minute is too short for polish, so you cannot over-think, and by the fifth or sixth panel the easy ideas are used up and you reach for less obvious ones.

## Why it works

The first idea is usually the most familiar, and people tend to stop there. Forcing eight different ideas pushes you past the familiar. The time pressure cuts self-criticism, and rough sketches are easy to discard, so you do not become attached to any one.

## When to use

- You have a design or product problem and a first idea you are not sure about
- A team needs several concrete alternatives in minutes
- You want to warm up before a longer design session
- Words are not enough and a rough picture would clarify things

## When not to use

- The issue is analytical, such as finding a cause; use [Five Whys](../root-cause/five-whys.md)
- No one in the group is willing to sketch (stick figures are fine, but they must try)
- You need depth on one idea instead of breadth

## How to apply

1. **Define the problem** in one sentence, ideally as a "How might we...?" question. See [How Might We](how-might-we.md).
2. **Prepare the paper.** Fold a sheet in half three times to make eight panels. Give each person a thick pen.
3. **Set a timer for 8 minutes**, with a signal every minute.
4. **Sketch one idea per minute.** Draw, do not write paragraphs. Short labels are fine.
5. **Fill all eight panels**, even if the later ones feel silly. Make each idea different from the last, not a small variation.
6. **Stop when the timer ends.** Do not refine.
7. **Share.** Each person shows their sheet and explains it in 30-60 seconds. Others stay silent and may note what they like.
8. **Pick.** Dot-vote, or note the strongest idea or part of an idea from each sheet.
9. **Take one or two ideas forward** into a more detailed sketch or prototype.

## Tips

- Use paper and a marker rather than a pen or a screen; the thick line stops you adding detail
- Mix the pattern: first eight on variations of the current idea, then eight radically different ones
- Give a prompt per panel if stuck: opposite, simpler, cheaper, for a different user
- Repeat a second round using the best idea from the first as the starting point

## Example: improving a restaurant's online ordering page

**Problem:** How might we make ordering takeaway on our website easier on a phone?

| Panel | Sketch |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Current layout with a larger "order" button |
| 2 | Menu as a single scrolling list with sticky categories |
| 3 | Photo-first grid of dishes |
| 4 | Chat-style ordering: "What would you like?" |
| 5 | "Order again" button for repeat customers |
| 6 | Three-step order: pick a dish, pick a time, pay |
| 7 | Barcode on takeaway bags that opens the reorder page |
| 8 | Voice ordering from a smart speaker |

**Result:** The team likes panels 5 and 6 and the sticky categories from panel 2. They combine them into one design and test it with five regular customers.

## Pitfalls

- **Making all eight the same.** Each panel should differ in kind, not just in detail.
- **Polishing.** If you are adding shading, you are too slow.
- **Using words only.** The value comes from showing, not describing.
- **Debating ideas.** Share first, discuss afterwards.
- **Stopping at generation.** Choose one or two and develop them.
- **Dismissing weak sketches.** Bad drawings can still hold good ideas.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Problem stated as one question
- [ ] Paper folded into 8 panels
- [ ] 8-minute timer, 1 minute per panel
- [ ] 8 different ideas
- [ ] Short share-out without debate
- [ ] One or two ideas chosen to develop
