# SCAMPER

> Run an existing idea through seven prompts, each one a different way to change it, and see what new ideas come out.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Idea generation |
| **Time needed** | 20-45 minutes, solo or in a group |
| **Best for** | Improving an existing product, service, or process rather than starting from nothing |

## What it is

SCAMPER is a checklist of seven idea-triggering verbs. You take something that already exists and ask what would happen if you applied each verb to it. The checklist was created by Bob Eberle in 1971, building on idea-spurring questions by Alex Osborn, the originator of brainstorming.

| Letter | Verb | Core question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S | Substitute | What can be replaced: a material, a person, a step, a rule? |
| C | Combine | What can be merged: two features, two products, two purposes? |
| A | Adapt | What else is like this? What could be borrowed from another field? |
| M | Modify | What can be made bigger, smaller, faster, slower, or different in form? |
| P | Put to another use | Who else could use this? What other problem could it solve? |
| E | Eliminate | What can be removed or simplified without losing the core value? |
| R | Reverse / Rearrange | What if the order, roles, or direction were flipped? |

## Why it works

Blank-page brainstorming is hard. SCAMPER gives each round a narrow, concrete question, so the mind has something to push against. Because the questions come from several directions, you cover options you would normally skip.

## When to use

- A product, service, or process works but could be better
- Brainstorming has stalled and ideas repeat
- You want a structured warm-up before a free session
- You need quick variations on a design or proposal

## When not to use

- There is nothing to start from; try [Lateral Thinking](lateral-thinking.md) first
- The real issue is an unknown cause; find it with [Five Whys](../root-cause/five-whys.md)
- You need a single decision, not a pool of options

## How to apply

1. **Name the subject.** Pick one product, service, or process, and describe it in a sentence.
2. **Write down what it does and why it exists.** This keeps ideas tied to real value.
3. **Take the verbs one at a time.** Spend 3-5 minutes on each.
4. **Answer the core question many ways.** Write every answer, however odd.
5. **Ask "what if?" on each answer.** Push it one step further.
6. **Mark the promising ones.** Look for ideas that are feasible, or that point to something feasible.
7. **Combine and refine.** Merge related ideas, then pick a few to test.

## Prompt questions by verb

| Verb | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Substitute | Who or what could replace it? A different material, place, or time? A different rule? |
| Combine | What could be bundled? What purposes could be merged? What teams or steps could join? |
| Adapt | What does this resemble? Which idea from another industry fits? What could we copy? |
| Modify | What if it were much larger or much smaller? Changed shape, colour, or frequency? |
| Put to another use | Who else needs this? Could a byproduct be useful? Could it work in a new setting? |
| Eliminate | What is not essential? What could be automated, shortened, or dropped? |
| Reverse / Rearrange | What if the steps ran backwards? What if customer and provider swapped roles? |

## Example: a neighbourhood coffee shop

**Situation:** A coffee shop has steady morning trade but empty afternoons.

| Verb | Idea |
| --- | --- |
| Substitute | Replace paper cups with a returnable cup scheme; replace afternoon coffee with iced drinks and tea |
| Combine | Combine the shop with a co-working space; combine coffee with a book swap |
| Adapt | Borrow the "happy hour" model from bars for a 3 pm to 5 pm discount |
| Modify | Smaller afternoon menu with faster prep; a larger "family size" pot for office orders |
| Put to another use | Rent the room for evening workshops and small meetings |
| Eliminate | Remove table service in the afternoon; remove rarely ordered items |
| Reverse | Customers brew their own with a guided tasting; the shop delivers to offices instead of waiting for customers |

**Result:** Three ideas selected for a one-month test: afternoon discount hours, a co-working corner, and office delivery of coffee pots.

## Working in a group

- Assign one verb per person first, then rotate
- Collect ideas on sticky notes and group them afterwards
- Keep criticism out until all seven verbs are done

## Pitfalls

- **Skipping verbs.** The uncomfortable ones, often Eliminate and Reverse, tend to produce the freshest ideas.
- **Stopping at the first answer.** Aim for several per verb.
- **Judging while generating.** Evaluate after all seven.
- **Losing the core purpose.** Keep the reason the thing exists in view.
- **Treating it as a one-off.** Re-run it on the best new idea.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Subject and purpose written down
- [ ] All seven verbs covered
- [ ] Several ideas per verb, unjudged
- [ ] Best ideas combined and shortlisted
- [ ] A cheap test planned
