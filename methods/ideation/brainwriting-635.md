# Brainwriting 6-3-5

> Six people each write three ideas in five minutes, pass the sheet on, and build on one another's ideas in silence.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Idea generation |
| **Time needed** | About 30 minutes for the rounds, plus 15-20 minutes to review |
| **Best for** | Groups where a few people dominate, or where people hold back ideas out loud |

## What it is

Brainwriting 6-3-5 is a structured written brainstorm. The name stands for **6** participants, **3** ideas each round, **5** minutes per round. It was developed by Bernd Rohrbach in Germany in the late 1960s.

Each person writes three ideas on a sheet, then passes it to the next person, who reads the ideas and adds three more, either new or building on what is already there. After six rounds, each sheet has gone through all six people. In theory, the group produces up to 108 ideas in about 30 minutes.

## Why it works

In a spoken brainstorm, one person talks at a time, louder voices dominate, and many people hold back for fear of judgment. In brainwriting everyone writes at once, so no one waits for a turn, and quiet participants contribute equally. Seeing others' ideas on the sheet provides a stimulus, so ideas build on each other without debate.

## When to use

- A group with mixed seniority or personalities
- You want many ideas in a short time
- Ideas should be built on, not argued over
- A topic where people may be reluctant to speak up

## When not to use

- Fewer than about four people; use a solo method such as [Crazy 8s](crazy-8s.md)
- The problem needs real-time discussion to clarify its meaning
- The group cannot work in silence or in writing

## How to apply

1. **Frame the problem** as a clear question. A "How might we...?" question works well; see [How Might We](how-might-we.md).
2. **Prepare the sheets.** One per person: a grid with 3 columns and 6 rows. Put the question at the top.
3. **Round 1 (5 minutes).** Each person writes three ideas in the first row. Short phrases are enough.
4. **Pass the sheet** clockwise to the next person.
5. **Round 2 (5 minutes).** Read the row above. Write three more ideas in the next row, either new or developments of earlier ones.
6. **Repeat** until each sheet has been passed through all participants, six rounds in total.
7. **Collect the sheets** and read them out or post them on a wall.
8. **Cluster and select.** Group similar ideas, merge duplicates, and vote or score to pick a shortlist.
9. **Plan next steps** for the best ideas.

## Adjusting the format

| Constraint | Adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| Fewer than six people | Reduce the number of rounds to match the number of people |
| More than six | Run parallel groups of six |
| Short on time | Use 3-3-3: three minutes per round |
| Remote team | Use a shared document with one section per person; rotate sections each round |
| Ideas too thin | Allow sketches or one sentence of detail |

## Example: reducing waste in an office kitchen

**Question:** How might we reduce disposable cups and plates in the office kitchen?

- **Round 1 ideas (Person A):** Free reusable mugs; a "cup tax" jar; put a sign at the cup dispenser.
- **Round 2 (Person B, building on A):** Replace the dispenser with mug hooks; personalised mugs with names; a weekly leaderboard for fewest disposables used.
- **Round 3 (Person C):** Dishwasher cycle every hour; team rewards when waste halves; stop buying disposables for a month and see.

**Result after six rounds:** About 90 ideas. After clustering, five themes: make reusables easy, make disposables harder to reach, track results, rewards, and awareness. The team votes and tests two for a month.

## Pitfalls

- **Talking during rounds.** Silence is the point.
- **Skipping the read.** Each round starts by reading the sheet; ideas build on earlier ones.
- **Only repeating.** Encourage variations and new angles, not copies.
- **Judging on the sheet.** No criticism or crossing out.
- **Stopping at generation.** The value comes after the rounds: clustering and picking.
- **Illegible writing.** Use block letters and short phrases.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Question framed clearly
- [ ] Sheets prepared with 3 columns and 6 rows
- [ ] Silent writing in timed rounds
- [ ] Each person builds on the sheet they receive
- [ ] Ideas clustered and shortlisted
- [ ] Next steps and owners agreed
