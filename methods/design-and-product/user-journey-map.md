# User Journey Map

> Lay out what a person does, thinks and feels step by step, to find where the experience breaks down.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Design and product |
| **Time needed** | 2-4 hours with a cross-functional team |
| **Best for** | Understanding an end-to-end experience across steps, channels and teams, and finding where to improve it |

## What it is

A user (or customer) journey map is a visual timeline of how a person goes through a process to reach a goal, such as buying a product, getting support or signing up. It shows the steps, what the person does, what they think and feel, where they come into contact with the organisation, and where the experience is poor. It has roots in service design, such as the service blueprint described by G. Lynn Shostack in the 1980s.

The map has stages across the top and layers down the side.

| Layer | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Stages | The phases of the journey (for example Aware, Consider, Buy, Use, Get help) |
| Actions | What the person does at each stage |
| Touchpoints | Where they meet the organisation (website, email, phone, store) |
| Thoughts and questions | What they are thinking |
| Emotions | How they feel, shown as a line from high to low |
| Pain points | Frustrations, delays, confusion |
| Opportunities | Ideas to improve each stage |

## Why it works

Organisations are structured in departments, but customers experience a journey. Each team may do its part well while the whole feels disjointed. A journey map puts the whole experience on one wall, so every team can see the handoffs, gaps and repeated work. The emotion line makes the low points easy to see and to agree on.

## When to use

- You want to improve an end-to-end experience
- Several teams touch the same customer and do not see the whole
- Complaints or drop-offs suggest a problem but not where
- You need a shared picture before redesigning

## When not to use

- You are exploring who the user is; start with research and an [Empathy Map](empathy-map.md)
- The journey is a single step
- The map would be built from assumptions with no research

## How to apply

1. **Choose the persona and the scenario.** One user type and one goal, such as "a new customer who wants to set up an account".
2. **Set the scope:** where the journey starts and ends.
3. **Gather evidence:** interviews, observation, analytics, support data. Include the people who deliver the experience.
4. **List the stages** in order, from the user's point of view.
5. **Add actions** at each stage: what the person does.
6. **Add touchpoints and channels:** where it happens, and who or what is involved.
7. **Add thoughts and questions** the person has.
8. **Plot the emotional curve:** high, neutral and low for each step.
9. **Mark pain points** and moments of delight.
10. **Find opportunities.** For each pain point, ask how the experience could be improved, or removed.
11. **Prioritise** the opportunities, for example with an [Impact-Effort Matrix](../prioritisation/impact-effort-matrix.md).
12. **Share and update** the map after changes are made.

## Current-state and future-state maps

| Type | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Current state | How it works today, with pain points |
| Future state | How you want it to work after changes |

Start with the current state. Build a future state once you know what to fix.

## Example: signing up for a gym membership

| Stage | Actions | Touchpoint | Thoughts | Emotion | Pain point | Opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Sees a friend's post; searches online | Social media, website | "Is it worth it?" | Curious | Prices hidden on the website | Show prices on the home page |
| Visit | Books a tour | Phone, front desk | "Will I feel out of place?" | Nervous | Long wait for a call back | Online booking with confirmation |
| Join | Fills in a form; pays | Front desk, paper form | "Why so many questions?" | Frustrated | Paper form, repeated data entry | Short online form, e-signature |
| First week | Attends a class | App, staff | "How do I book?" | Confused | The app crashes when booking | Fix booking flow; welcome email |
| Month 3 | Considers cancelling | Email, phone | "Am I using this?" | Discouraged | Cancelling requires a visit | Easy online pause and cancel; check-in message |

**Biggest low point:** Joining and the first week. The team prioritises the online form and the booking bug.

## Pitfalls

- **Building it from assumptions.** Base it on research and data.
- **Too broad.** One persona and one scenario per map.
- **Too detailed.** The value comes from the pattern, not every click.
- **Ignoring the back stage.** Include the internal steps that cause problems.
- **A poster that is never used.** Link each opportunity to an owner and a decision.
- **Forgetting good moments.** Protect what already works.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] One persona and one scenario
- [ ] Based on evidence
- [ ] Stages, actions, touchpoints, thoughts and emotions filled in
- [ ] Pain points and opportunities marked
- [ ] Opportunities prioritised and owned
- [ ] Map updated after changes
