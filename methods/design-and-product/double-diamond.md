# Double Diamond

> Open up and then narrow down twice: first to find the right problem, then to find the right solution.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Design and product |
| **Time needed** | A framework for a project of days to months |
| **Best for** | Structuring a project so that the team explores widely before it commits, both on the problem and on the answer |

## What it is

The Double Diamond is a design process model made popular by the British Design Council in 2005. It shows a project as two diamonds side by side. In each diamond, thinking first **diverges** (opens up to explore many possibilities) and then **converges** (narrows down to a choice).

| Diamond | Phase | Mode | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First: the right problem | Discover | Diverge | Explore the situation widely; gather insights |
| First: the right problem | Define | Converge | Make sense of what you found; choose the problem to solve |
| Second: the right solution | Develop | Diverge | Explore many possible solutions; prototype |
| Second: the right solution | Deliver | Converge | Test, refine and launch the chosen solution |

```text
 Discover (open) -> Define (narrow)      Develop (open) -> Deliver (narrow)
 |--- diamond 1: the right problem ---|  |--- diamond 2: the right solution ---|
```

The model builds on earlier work on divergent and convergent thinking. The Design Council updated it in 2019 as its Framework for Innovation, adding principles and methods to each stage.

## Why it works

Teams often jump to a solution before understanding the problem, or stay open for so long that nothing is chosen. The two diamonds make the rhythm explicit: it is time to widen, and then it is time to decide. Separating "the right problem" from "the right solution" prevents polishing the wrong thing.

## When to use

- At the start of a project, to plan how to work
- A team keeps jumping to solutions
- A team keeps exploring and never decides
- You want a shared language for process and progress

## When not to use

- The problem is already defined and validated
- The task is routine delivery
- You are tempted to treat the stages as a rigid timetable

## How to apply

1. **Discover (diverge).** Research broadly: talk to users, observe, review data, look at other fields. Resist solutions. Use methods such as [Empathy Map](empathy-map.md) and [Jobs To Be Done](jobs-to-be-done.md).
2. **Define (converge).** Group what you found into insights. Choose the problem or opportunity to focus on, and write it as a brief or a [How Might We](../ideation/how-might-we.md) question.
3. **Develop (diverge).** Generate many solutions with [Crazy 8s](../ideation/crazy-8s.md), [Brainwriting 6-3-5](../ideation/brainwriting-635.md) or [SCAMPER](../ideation/scamper.md). Build rough prototypes.
4. **Deliver (converge).** Test with users, narrow to the best option, refine it and launch.
5. **Loop back** when needed. If testing shows the problem was wrong, return to Discover or Define.

## What to produce at each stage

| Stage | Typical output |
| --- | --- |
| Discover | Research notes, observations, a wide set of insights |
| Define | A clear problem statement or design brief; success criteria |
| Develop | A set of prototypes and early test results |
| Deliver | A tested solution, a launch plan, measures of success |

## Example: reducing missed medical appointments

| Stage | Activity | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | Interview 15 patients who missed appointments; review booking data; shadow the reception | Many forgot, some could not travel, some found it hard to cancel |
| Define | Cluster insights | Problem: "Patients who cannot attend find it hard to tell us, so slots are wasted" |
| Develop | Sketch ideas: one-tap cancel by text, a waiting list, a phone call back, a travel voucher. Prototype the text flow | Three prototypes |
| Deliver | Test the text flow for a month in one clinic; refine wording; roll out | Missed appointments fall and freed slots go to the waiting list |

**Note:** The team first assumed forgetting was the problem and had planned reminders. The Discover and Define stages showed that the real issue was how hard it was to cancel.

## Pitfalls

- **Skipping the first diamond.** The most common error and the most costly.
- **Diverging forever.** Set a time limit and a way to choose.
- **Converging too early.** Keep options open until you have enough insight.
- **Treating it as linear.** Go back when you learn something.
- **Confusing the model with the work.** The diamonds are a guide, not a form to fill in.
- **No users in the second diamond.** Test with real people.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Discover: broad research, no solutions yet
- [ ] Define: one clear problem or brief
- [ ] Develop: many options and prototypes
- [ ] Deliver: tested, refined and launched
- [ ] Time boxes for each phase
- [ ] Loops back allowed
