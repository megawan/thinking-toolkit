# Empathy Map

> Put what a person says, thinks, does and feels on one page to understand them beyond what they tell you.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Design and product |
| **Time needed** | 30-60 minutes per map |
| **Best for** | Building a shared understanding of a user or customer, after research and before designing |

## What it is

An empathy map is a canvas for organising what you know about a person or a group. It was developed by Dave Gray at the consultancy XPLANE and is widely used in design thinking. The classic version has four quadrants around the person.

| Quadrant | What it holds | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Says | Quotes and phrases the person uses | Interviews |
| Thinks | What they may be thinking but do not say | Inference from words and behaviour |
| Does | Actions and behaviours you saw | Observation |
| Feels | Emotions, worries, hopes | Inference from tone and context |

A later version adds **Sees** and **Hears** (what they see and hear in their environment) and two summary areas: **Pains** (fears, frustrations, obstacles) and **Gains** (wants, needs, measures of success).

## Why it works

Teams often work from assumptions about users. Writing down evidence in the four quadrants separates observed facts (says, does) from inferences (thinks, feels), and highlights gaps and contradictions between them. Gaps between what people say and what they do are often where the insights are. A single page also gives the whole team a common picture of the user.

## When to use

- After user interviews or observation, to synthesise what you learned
- A team debates what users want without evidence
- You need to share user understanding with people who did not do the research
- Before writing a brief or generating ideas

## When not to use

- You have no research; it will fill with guesses
- You need quantitative data, such as how many people share a view
- A single map is used to represent everyone; separate them by user type

## How to apply

1. **Choose the user** or user segment. One map per type.
2. **Gather evidence:** interview notes, observation notes, quotes, support tickets.
3. **Draw the canvas.** The person in the centre, with the quadrants around them. Add Pains and Gains below if you use them.
4. **Fill Says and Does** with direct quotes and observed behaviour. Use one note per item.
5. **Infer Thinks and Feels,** based on the evidence. Mark inferences as such.
6. **Add Sees and Hears,** if used: their environment, influencers and messages.
7. **Summarise Pains and Gains.**
8. **Look for tensions.** What does the person say that differs from what they do? What do they feel that they do not say?
9. **Draw insights and questions.** Mark what you still need to find out.
10. **Use it** in the next step: a problem statement, a persona or a [How Might We](../ideation/how-might-we.md) question.

## Prompts for each quadrant

| Quadrant | Prompts |
| --- | --- |
| Says | What did they tell us? Which words do they repeat? |
| Thinks | What matters to them? What do they worry about but hide? |
| Does | What do they do first? What workarounds do they use? |
| Feels | When were they frustrated, anxious, pleased? What triggered it? |
| Pains | What gets in their way? What do they fear? |
| Gains | What does success look like to them? |

## Example: a first-time home buyer

| Quadrant | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Says | "I just want to know if I can afford it." "The forms are confusing." |
| Thinks | "Will I be turned down? Am I being taken advantage of?" |
| Does | Uses three calculators and a spreadsheet; asks a friend; avoids phone calls with banks |
| Feels | Anxious about money, embarrassed about not knowing terms, excited about owning |
| Sees | Adverts showing smiling couples; friends posting house photos |
| Hears | Mixed advice from family; "interest rates are going up" |
| Pains | Jargon, hidden fees, fear of making a big mistake |
| Gains | A clear monthly figure, a person who explains, confidence in the choice |

**Tension:** The person says they want to know if they can afford it, but avoids contacting banks because they feel embarrassed.

**Insight:** An early, private, jargon-free affordability check, with no commitment, would meet the need.

## Pitfalls

- **Filling it with assumptions.** Every note should link back to research.
- **Mixing user types.** One map per segment.
- **Stopping at description.** The value is in the tensions and insights.
- **Treating inferences as facts.** Mark them.
- **Doing it alone.** A shared session builds shared understanding.
- **One-off.** Update the map as you learn.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] One user segment per map
- [ ] Notes based on evidence
- [ ] Says and Does separated from Thinks and Feels
- [ ] Pains and Gains summarised
- [ ] Tensions and insights marked
- [ ] Open questions listed
