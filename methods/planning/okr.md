# OKR

> Set an inspiring objective and a few measurable key results, review them regularly, and align the team around them.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Planning and goals |
| **Time needed** | 2-4 hours to set; 30 minutes per week to review |
| **Best for** | Teams and organisations that need focus and alignment over a quarter or a year |

## What it is

OKR stands for Objectives and Key Results. An **objective** says what you want to achieve, in words that motivate. **Key results** are 2-5 measurable outcomes that show whether you got there.

The method was developed by Andy Grove at Intel in the 1970s, built on Peter Drucker's management by objectives. John Doerr brought it to Google in 1999 and described it in his 2018 book *Measure What Matters*.

| Part | Description | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective | Qualitative, ambitious, time-bound | Make our onboarding delightful |
| Key result | Quantitative, specific, shows progress | Raise activation from 35% to 50% |
| Initiative | The work you do to move a key result | Redesign the first-run checklist |

## Why it works

A goal without a measure is a wish, and a measure without a goal is a number. OKRs link the two. Having only a few objectives forces focus. Writing them down and sharing them makes priorities visible, and regular check-ins keep them alive. Ambitious targets encourage reaching beyond what seems safe.

## When to use

- A team needs to focus on a few outcomes
- Different groups need to pull in the same direction
- You want to measure progress by outcomes rather than activity
- A new period (quarter or year) is starting

## When not to use

- You need to manage routine work and quality; use normal metrics
- Goals are mostly fixed tasks with no uncertainty; a plan is enough
- OKRs would be used as a tool for individual performance reviews; this discourages ambition

## How to apply

1. **Start from strategy.** What matters most this period?
2. **Write 1-3 objectives.** Each should be inspiring, clear, and achievable within the period.
3. **Write 2-5 key results per objective.** Each must be measurable, with a start value and a target, such as "from X to Y".
4. **Check that key results measure outcomes,** not activities. "Launch a new tutorial" is an activity; "raise activation to 50%" is an outcome.
5. **Choose a stretch level.** Decide which are committed (expected to reach 100%) and which are aspirational (reaching about 70% is good).
6. **Share and align.** Teams read each other's OKRs and check that they support the higher-level ones.
7. **Plan initiatives** that you believe will move the key results.
8. **Check in weekly.** Update the progress and confidence. Raise blockers early.
9. **Score at the end** on a scale of 0 to 1. Reflect on what you learned.
10. **Start the next cycle** with what you learned.

## Writing good objectives and key results

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Improve the website | Make our website the easiest place to learn about our service |
| Launch the new feature | Reach 1,000 weekly users of the new feature by the end of the quarter |
| Increase sales | Raise monthly sales from $80,000 to $100,000 |
| Be better at support | Cut median response time from 6 hours to 2 hours |

## Example

**Objective:** Make onboarding delightful for new customers.

| Key result | Start | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activation rate (new users who finish setup in week 1) | 35% | 50% |
| Median time to create the first project | 3 days | 1 day |
| New-user satisfaction score | 20 | 35 |

**Initiatives:** A guided setup checklist, templates for common cases, and a welcome message from a real person.

**Mid-quarter check:** Activation is 42% and time to first project is 1.5 days, but the satisfaction score has not moved. The team reviews comments and finds that support replies are slow, so adds an initiative on that.

**Scoring:** At the end, activation 48% (score about 0.85), time to first project 1 day (1.0), satisfaction 28 (about 0.5). The average is 0.78, which suggests a good, ambitious set.

## Pitfalls

- **Too many OKRs.** More than three objectives dilutes focus.
- **Activities as key results.** Measure outcomes.
- **Sandbagging.** Setting easy targets to look good.
- **Tying OKRs directly to pay.** It leads to cautious goals.
- **Set and forget.** Without weekly check-ins, OKRs die.
- **No link to strategy.** OKRs that do not support the main goals waste effort.
- **Unmeasurable key results.** If you cannot tell whether it moved, rewrite it.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] 1-3 objectives, inspiring and clear
- [ ] 2-5 measurable key results each, with start and target
- [ ] Outcomes, not activities
- [ ] Committed and aspirational marked
- [ ] Aligned with other teams
- [ ] Weekly check-ins scheduled
- [ ] Scored and reviewed at the end
