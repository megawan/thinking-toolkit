# Backcasting

> Define the future you want, then work backwards to find the steps that get you there from today.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Planning and goals |
| **Time needed** | 1-3 hours, solo or with a team |
| **Best for** | Ambitious, long-term goals where extending today's trends will not get you there |

## What it is

Backcasting is a planning method that starts from a desired future state and works backwards to the present, identifying what must happen at each stage. It contrasts with **forecasting**, which starts from today and projects forward.

The term was introduced by John B. Robinson in the early 1980s for energy policy planning. It is now used in sustainability, strategy, product planning and personal goals.

| | Forecasting | Backcasting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starts from | The present | A defined future |
| Asks | What is likely to happen? | What would need to happen to reach this? |
| Strength | Realistic about trends | Reveals big changes and the order of steps |
| Risk | Locks in current limits | Can ignore constraints if not checked |

## Why it works

If you plan forwards, you tend to extend what you already do, which leads to small improvements. Starting from the destination lets you ask whether the current path can reach it at all, and shows which steps must come first. It turns a distant, vague goal into a sequence of nearer ones, each of which has a clear purpose.

## When to use

- A large goal is far away and the path is unclear
- Incremental improvement will not be enough
- You need milestones and an order of steps
- A team needs a shared picture of the destination

## When not to use

- The goal is near and the steps are obvious
- The future is too uncertain to describe, and you need to experiment first
- You need a forecast of what will happen, not a plan to make it happen

## How to apply

1. **Define the future state.** Describe it in concrete terms, with a date: what is true, who is affected, how you can tell. Aim for a vivid, measurable picture.
2. **Describe the present** honestly: where you are now, what you have, what is missing.
3. **Work backward from the goal.** Ask: what must be true just before this? Write that as the previous milestone. Repeat until you reach today.
4. **Check the order.** Does each milestone enable the next? Are any dependencies missing?
5. **Find the gaps.** Compare each milestone with today's resources, skills and constraints. Where is the biggest jump?
6. **List actions** for each gap, starting with the nearest milestone.
7. **Test feasibility.** Which milestone is the riskiest? Is the timeline realistic? If not, adjust the date, scope or route.
8. **Set the first step** and a review date. Revisit the plan as you learn.

## Prompt questions

- What would have to be true for this to succeed?
- What is the last thing that must happen before we reach the goal?
- What is the biggest obstacle between here and there?
- What do we need that we do not yet have?
- Which step could we start this week?

## Example: launching an online course

**Goal:** On 1 September, the course is live with 100 paying students.

| Date | Milestone (worked backwards) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 September | Course live; 100 students enrolled |
| August | Marketing campaign running; waiting list of 300 people |
| July | Beta test with 15 students; improvements made |
| June | All lessons recorded and edited; platform chosen and set up |
| May | Scripts written and slides finished |
| April | Outline validated with 10 interviews of target students |
| Now (March) | Decide the topic, book interview slots, block writing time |

**Gap analysis:** The biggest jump is from zero audience to 300 on the waiting list by August. The plan adds a weekly newsletter starting in April, so the audience builds during production.

**Result:** The first step is clear: schedule the interviews this week.

## Example: a personal goal

**Goal:** Move to a new city for a new job within 12 months.

Working backwards: start the job and have housing by month 12, accept an offer by month 9, interview from month 6, apply from month 4, update CV and portfolio by month 3, decide target roles and cities this month.

## Pitfalls

- **A vague destination.** If you cannot describe it, you cannot work back from it.
- **Ignoring constraints.** A beautiful plan that cannot be funded is a daydream.
- **A single route.** Consider alternatives if a milestone looks too risky.
- **Skipping the present.** The gap analysis is where the real work appears.
- **Rigid plans.** Revisit the plan as facts change.
- **No first step.** End with something you can do this week.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Future state described concretely, with a date
- [ ] Present state described honestly
- [ ] Milestones worked backwards
- [ ] Dependencies and order checked
- [ ] Gaps and risky milestones identified
- [ ] First step and review date set
