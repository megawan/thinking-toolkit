# SMART Goals

> Make a goal Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound, so you can tell what to do and whether you did it.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Planning and goals |
| **Time needed** | 10-20 minutes per goal |
| **Best for** | Turning a vague wish into a clear goal for yourself or a team |

## What it is

SMART is a checklist for writing goals. It comes from a 1981 article by George T. Doran in *Management Review*. The letters have several variants. The most common version is:

| Letter | Meaning | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S | Specific | What exactly will be done, by whom, and for what? |
| M | Measurable | How will we know we have achieved it? |
| A | Achievable | Is it possible with the resources and time we have? |
| R | Relevant | Does it matter for our larger aims? |
| T | Time-bound | By when? |

Doran's original wording used "Assignable" for A and "Realistic" for R.

## Why it works

A vague goal, such as "get fit", cannot be started, measured or finished. Making each part explicit removes the guesswork. A measurable target shows progress, a deadline creates a rhythm, and a check for relevance and feasibility stops you from committing to the wrong or impossible thing.

## When to use

- You have a wish or ambition and need to make it actionable
- A team must agree what "done" means
- You are writing objectives for a project or a year
- You want to review whether a goal is clear

## When not to use

- You want to explore possibilities; start with a creative method first
- The outcome is uncertain and you need to learn; set a learning goal
- You are tempted to use a rigid target where flexibility is needed

## How to apply

1. **Write the first draft** of the goal in your own words.
2. **Make it specific.** Say what, who, where and why. Replace vague verbs, such as "improve", with concrete ones.
3. **Add a measure.** Choose a number, a yes or no condition, or a clear deliverable.
4. **Check achievability.** Is it a stretch but possible with your time, skills and resources? If not, scale it or split it.
5. **Check relevance.** Why does it matter? Which bigger goal does it support?
6. **Set a deadline,** and consider milestones along the way.
7. **Write the final sentence** that includes all parts.
8. **Plan the first action** and the date to review progress.

## Rewriting vague goals

| Vague | SMART |
| --- | --- |
| Get fit | Run a 5 km race in under 30 minutes by 31 March by training three times a week, tracked in a running app |
| Save money | Save $3,000 for an emergency fund by 30 September by moving $250 on payday each month |
| Improve customer service | Reduce median support response time from 6 hours to 2 hours by the end of Q3 by adding an evening shift |
| Learn Spanish | Reach conversation level (B1) by December by studying 30 minutes a day and having one weekly class |
| Write a book | Finish a 60,000-word first draft by 1 June by writing 500 words on each weekday |

## Example: a team goal

**First draft:** Make our documentation better.

| Letter | Check | Revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific | Which documentation? Better how? | The setup and troubleshooting guides |
| Measurable | What is better? | Support tickets about setup fall by 30% |
| Achievable | Do we have time? | Two writers for two days a week; yes |
| Relevant | Why does it matter? | Setup tickets are the largest ticket category |
| Time-bound | When? | By the end of next quarter |

**SMART goal:** Rewrite the setup and troubleshooting guides by 30 June, with the aim of cutting setup-related support tickets by 30% by the end of the quarter.

## Variants

| Variant | Adds |
| --- | --- |
| SMARTER | Evaluate and Reassess: review the goal regularly |
| Learning goal | Replaces "achieve" with "find out", when the outcome is unknown |

## Pitfalls

- **Meaningless metrics.** A number that does not reflect the aim leads you in the wrong direction.
- **Too easy.** Achievable is not the same as trivial.
- **Too rigid.** Goals may need to change when circumstances do.
- **Output without outcome.** "Write 10 posts" is not the same as "grow readers".
- **Too many goals.** A handful, not dozens.
- **No review.** Check progress at set times.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Specific: what, who, why
- [ ] Measurable: a number or clear condition
- [ ] Achievable: resources and time checked
- [ ] Relevant: linked to a bigger aim
- [ ] Time-bound: a deadline and milestones
- [ ] First action and review date set
