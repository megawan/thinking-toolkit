# 10-10-10

> Ask how you will feel about a decision in 10 minutes, 10 months and 10 years.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Decision making |
| **Time needed** | 5-15 minutes |
| **Best for** | Choices where short-term feelings may be crowding out long-term consequences |

## What it is

10-10-10 is a quick decision aid popularised by the writer Suzy Welch, in her 2009 book *10-10-10: A Life-Transforming Idea*. Before deciding, you consider the consequences of each option on three time scales: the next 10 minutes, the next 10 months, and the next 10 years.

## Why it works

Decisions made under pressure are dominated by how things feel right now: relief, embarrassment, excitement, fear. Looking at several horizons separates the immediate feeling from the lasting effect. Often the option that feels worst in 10 minutes is the best in 10 years, and the reverse.

## When to use

- You are about to react in the heat of the moment
- A short-term pull conflicts with a longer-term goal
- A personal or work decision where the short and long term differ
- You need a fast gut check before a bigger analysis

## When not to use

- The decision needs data or analysis; try [Cost-Benefit Analysis](cost-benefit-analysis.md)
- The choice is trivial
- The time scales are unrealistic for the decision; adapt them (see below)

## How to apply

1. **State the decision** and the options, including doing nothing.
2. **For each option, ask three questions:**
   - How will I feel, and what will happen, in 10 minutes?
   - How will it look in 10 months?
   - How will it look in 10 years?
3. **Write short answers.** One or two lines each.
4. **Compare across the horizons.** Where do the options flip?
5. **Weigh by importance.** Decide how much each horizon matters for this decision.
6. **Choose,** and plan to deal with the short-term cost if the long-term benefit wins.

## Adjusting the time scales

| Decision | Possible scales |
| --- | --- |
| A quick reply to an email | 10 minutes, 10 days, 10 months |
| A career or life choice | 10 days, 10 months, 10 years |
| A business purchase | 10 weeks, 10 months, 10 quarters |
| A relationship issue | 10 minutes, 10 weeks, 10 years |

Keep the idea of three very different horizons; the numbers can change.

## Example: replying to a harsh email from a colleague

**Decision:** Send an angry reply now, wait until tomorrow, or ask to talk.

| Option | 10 minutes | 10 months | 10 years |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Angry reply | Feels satisfying | Strained working relationship, possible complaint | Probably forgotten, but a record of poor conduct |
| Wait until tomorrow | Uncomfortable, unresolved | Likely calm and professional | No effect |
| Ask to talk | Slight anxiety | A clearer understanding and a better working relationship | No effect or a lasting good contact |

**Decision:** Ask for a short call tomorrow, and send a brief acknowledgement today.

## Example: staying late to finish a project vs. going to a family event

**Decision:** Work late on a deadline or attend a family dinner.

- **10 minutes:** Work feels urgent; dinner feels like a sacrifice.
- **10 months:** The deadline is long past; the dinner is remembered.
- **10 years:** The project is a small item on a CV; the family event may be a lasting memory.

**Decision:** Attend, and work a focused hour afterwards to meet the deadline.

## Pitfalls

- **Treating the 10-minute view as always wrong.** Sometimes the short-term matters, for example in a real emergency.
- **Guessing wildly about 10 years.** Keep to what is plausible.
- **Ignoring the middle.** The 10-month view is often the most practical and the most overlooked.
- **Stopping at feelings.** Add facts and effects, not only emotions.
- **Using it for complex decisions alone.** Add other methods for high stakes.
- **Skipping "do nothing".** Not deciding is also a choice with outcomes on each horizon.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Options listed, including doing nothing
- [ ] Three horizons answered for each option
- [ ] Differences between horizons noted
- [ ] Importance of each horizon weighed
- [ ] Short-term cost planned for
