# Six Thinking Hats

> Look at a problem from six separate angles, one at a time, so a group stops arguing across each other.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Decision making |
| **Time needed** | 30-90 minutes, best with a group of 3-8 |
| **Best for** | Meetings, decisions, and reviews where opinions clash or one viewpoint dominates |

## What it is

Six Thinking Hats is a framework by Edward de Bono (1985). Each of six coloured "hats" stands for a distinct mode of thinking. Everyone wears the same hat at the same time, so the group explores facts, feelings, risks, benefits, and ideas in turn instead of mixing them in one tangled discussion.

## Why it works

In ordinary discussion, people argue from different modes at once: one person states facts, another worries about risk, a third pitches a new idea, and each is heard as an attack on the others. Separating the modes removes the clash. It also gives quieter people a reason to speak and makes the critic's role a scheduled step rather than a personality.

## The six hats

| Hat | Mode | Focus | Typical questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| White | Facts | Information and data | What do we know? What do we need to find out? |
| Red | Feelings | Intuition and emotion, no justification needed | How do I feel about this? What is my gut reaction? |
| Black | Caution | Risks, problems, why it might fail | What could go wrong? What are the weaknesses? |
| Yellow | Optimism | Benefits, value, why it might work | What are the advantages? Who gains? |
| Green | Creativity | New ideas, alternatives | What else could we do? What if...? |
| Blue | Process | Managing the thinking itself | What is our goal? Which hat next? What have we decided? |

## When to use

- A group keeps arguing without progress
- A decision needs balanced consideration
- One strong voice dominates the room
- You want to review a plan thoroughly
- You are thinking alone and want to avoid defaulting to your usual style

## When not to use

- A quick, low-stakes decision
- Participants refuse to follow the structure
- You need deep technical analysis rather than a broad review

## How to apply

1. **State the topic** in one sentence.
2. **Pick a facilitator** (the blue hat) who keeps time and calls the hats.
3. **Choose a sequence** (see below) and a time limit per hat, typically 3-5 minutes.
4. **Run each hat.** Everyone thinks in that mode only. Write points on a shared board.
5. **Keep the rules.** No arguing between hats; a Black comment during Yellow is parked until its turn.
6. **Finish with Blue.** Summarise what emerged, decide, and agree next steps.

## Suggested sequences

| Purpose | Order |
| --- | --- |
| Start a new idea | Blue, White, Green, Yellow, Black, Red, Blue |
| Evaluate a proposal | Blue, White, Yellow, Black, Green, Red, Blue |
| Quick check | Blue, Red, White, Black, Yellow, Blue |
| Solve a problem | Blue, White, Green, Black, Yellow, Red, Blue |
| Review a past decision | Blue, White, Red, Black, Yellow, Blue |

## Example

**Topic:** Should we move our team to a four-day work week?

- **White:** Output has been steady for two quarters. Two clients require Friday availability. Survey: 70% of staff are in favour.
- **Red:** Staff feel excited. Managers feel uneasy about losing control.
- **Black:** Customer response times could suffer. Deadlines might slip. Not every role can compress hours.
- **Yellow:** Better retention, easier hiring, possible rise in focus.
- **Green:** Rotate days off. Try a 3-month pilot. Keep a small rotating Friday support team.
- **Blue:** Decision: run a 3-month pilot with two clients covered by rotation; review metrics in month 3.

## Using it alone

Write each hat's thoughts on a separate page. Set a timer per hat. The value is in making yourself think in a mode you normally skip, often Red or Green for analytical people, and Black for optimists.

## Pitfalls

- **Mixing hats.** The main rule is one mode at a time. Park off-hat comments.
- **Skipping Black or Yellow.** Both are needed for a balanced view.
- **Justifying Red.** Feelings in the red hat need no reasons.
- **Treating hats as personality types.** They are modes anyone can switch between.
- **No Blue hat.** Without a facilitator, the process drifts.
- **Ending without a decision.** Close with concrete next steps.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Topic and goal agreed
- [ ] Facilitator chosen
- [ ] Sequence and time per hat set
- [ ] Points captured on a shared board
- [ ] Off-hat comments parked
- [ ] Decision and next steps recorded
