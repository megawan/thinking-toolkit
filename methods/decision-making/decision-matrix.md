# Decision Matrix

> Compare options against the criteria that matter, with weights, so a choice rests on stated reasons and not on whoever argues best.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Decision making |
| **Time needed** | 20-45 minutes, solo or with a group |
| **Best for** | Choosing between several options with several competing criteria |

## What it is

A decision matrix, also called a weighted scoring model or Pugh matrix, is a table. Options sit in the rows, criteria in the columns, and each cell holds a score. Criteria carry weights that show how much they matter. The weighted scores are added up for each option.

## Why it works

Hard decisions mix many factors, and the mind fixes on a few. Listing criteria in advance makes the trade-offs explicit and stops one vivid factor from taking over. Weights force a discussion of what actually matters. In a group, the matrix surfaces disagreement about criteria, which is more useful than disagreement about the answer.

## When to use

- Three or more options and several criteria
- A group must agree on a choice and explain it later
- The choice is significant enough to justify some effort
- Gut feeling and analysis point in different directions

## When not to use

- Choosing between two options on one criterion
- The decision is quick and easy to reverse
- A key factor cannot be scored; treat it as a must-have filter instead

## How to apply

1. **State the decision** and the goal in one sentence.
2. **List the options.** Include "do nothing" if it is real.
3. **Set must-haves.** Remove options that fail a hard requirement, such as budget or legal limits.
4. **List criteria.** Choose what matters: cost, quality, speed, risk, fit, effort. Keep it to 4-8.
5. **Weight the criteria.** Give each a weight, for example 1 to 5, or percentages that sum to 100. Agree on weights before scoring.
6. **Score each option** on each criterion, for example 1 to 5, with a clear scale. Higher should always mean better; for cost, higher means cheaper.
7. **Multiply and add.** Weight times score per cell, then total per option.
8. **Sanity-check.** Does the top result feel right? If not, find out why: a missing criterion, a wrong weight, or gut bias.
9. **Test sensitivity.** Change the weights a little. If the winner changes, the options are close; discuss further.
10. **Decide and record.** Note the reasons and who agreed.

## Example: choosing a project tool

**Decision:** Select a project management tool for a team of ten.

| Criterion | Weight | Tool A | Tool B | Tool C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ease of use | 5 | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| Cost | 4 | 3 | 5 | 2 |
| Integrations | 3 | 5 | 3 | 3 |
| Reporting | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| Support | 2 | 4 | 2 | 4 |

**Weighted totals:**

| Tool | Calculation | Total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 5x4 + 4x3 + 3x5 + 2x3 + 2x4 | 61 |
| B | 5x3 + 4x5 + 3x3 + 2x4 + 2x2 | 56 |
| C | 5x5 + 4x2 + 3x3 + 2x4 + 2x4 | 58 |

**Result:** Tool A leads Tool C by only 3 points, so the result is close. Raising the weight of cost from 4 to 5 widens A's lead (A: 64, B: 61, C: 60), so the ranking holds. The team confirms that A's price fits the budget, then decides.

## Tips

- Agree on criteria and weights before looking at the options, to avoid rigging
- Score independently first in a group, then compare
- Define what each score means ("5 = meets all needs without workaround")
- Keep unscored factors, such as team morale, visible beside the matrix

## Pitfalls

- **Too many criteria.** The matrix gets noisy and hides what matters.
- **Overlapping criteria.** "Quality" and "reliability" can double-count the same thing.
- **False precision.** Scores are judgments; a 61 versus 58 is a tie.
- **Reverse-engineering.** Adjusting weights until the favourite wins defeats the purpose.
- **Ignoring risk.** Add risk as a criterion, or as a must-have filter.
- **Hiding the matrix's limits.** It supports a decision; it does not replace judgment.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Decision and goal stated
- [ ] Must-haves applied first
- [ ] 4-8 distinct criteria
- [ ] Weights agreed before scoring
- [ ] Scale defined and consistent
- [ ] Sensitivity checked
- [ ] Reasons recorded
