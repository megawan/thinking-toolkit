# Wheel of Life

> Rate how satisfied you are in each area of your life on a circle, and see where attention is needed.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Reflection and learning |
| **Time needed** | 20-40 minutes; repeat every few months |
| **Best for** | Taking stock of your life, noticing imbalance, and choosing what to work on next |

## What it is

The Wheel of Life is a self-assessment tool widely used in coaching. You draw a circle divided into segments like the spokes of a wheel. Each segment is an area of life, and you score your current satisfaction from the centre (low) to the rim (high). The shape of the filled-in wheel shows how balanced your life feels.

Its origin in modern coaching is often attributed to the motivational author Paul J. Meyer in the 1960s. It is a different tool from the Buddhist wheel of life, with which it should not be confused.

## Typical areas

There is no fixed list. A common set of eight is:

| Area | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Career and work | Satisfaction with your job, progress, purpose |
| Finances | Income, savings, security |
| Health and fitness | Energy, sleep, exercise, physical health |
| Family and friends | Quality of close relationships and time with them |
| Partner or romance | The relationship, or the wish for one |
| Personal growth | Learning, skills, self-development |
| Fun and recreation | Hobbies, rest, enjoyment |
| Physical environment | Home, workplace, surroundings |

You can rename, add or remove areas to fit your life, for example spirituality, community or contribution.

## Why it works

Most of us notice only the area that is loudest, usually work or a current worry. Putting all areas on one picture reveals which have been neglected. Scoring makes feelings concrete enough to compare, and the visual shape prompts a question: what would a more balanced wheel look like? It also gives a starting point for goals and a baseline to compare with in the future.

## When to use

- At the start of a year, a new phase, or a transition
- When something feels off but you cannot say what
- Before setting goals or priorities
- In coaching or a reflective conversation

## When not to use

- During a crisis, when practical help matters more
- When you want a rigorous measure of wellbeing; this is a reflection tool, not a test
- If it leads you to judge yourself harshly; use it with kindness

## How to apply

1. **Draw the wheel.** A large circle divided into 8 segments (or your own number). Mark ten rings from the centre outward, where 1 is the centre and 10 is the rim.
2. **Name the areas** and write one on each segment.
3. **Score your satisfaction** in each, from 1 (very dissatisfied) to 10 (fully satisfied). Go with your first feeling. Shade each segment up to its score.
4. **Look at the shape.** Is the wheel round, or lopsided? Which areas are low, and which are high?
5. **Reflect.** For the lowest areas, ask: what is behind this score? What would a higher score look like (a "7" or "8")? Is this area important to me right now?
6. **Notice connections.** Does a low score in one area affect others, such as poor sleep affecting work?
7. **Choose focus.** Pick one or two areas to work on over the next period. Be realistic.
8. **Set one small action per area.** For example, a walk three times a week, or a monthly budget review.
9. **Repeat in three to six months,** and compare the wheels.

## Reflection questions

- What is working well in this area, and why?
- What would make this score one point higher?
- What do I need to stop, start or ask for?
- Is a low score a problem, or a choice for this stage of life?
- Which area, if improved, would lift others?

## Example

| Area | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Career and work | 8 |
| Finances | 6 |
| Health and fitness | 3 |
| Family and friends | 5 |
| Partner or romance | 7 |
| Personal growth | 5 |
| Fun and recreation | 3 |
| Physical environment | 7 |

**Reading:** Work is strong, but health and fun are low. The person has been working long hours.

**Reflection:** "Health at 3 affects my energy at work. A 6 would mean two walks and earlier nights."

**Actions for the next three months:**

- Health: walk for 30 minutes on three days a week, and be in bed by 23:00 on weeknights
- Fun: book one social or hobby evening every two weeks
- Family and friends: a call with a friend each weekend

**Review:** After three months, the new wheel shows health at 5 and fun at 5, while work stays at 8.

## Pitfalls

- **Overthinking the numbers.** The pattern matters more than the exact score.
- **Comparing with others.** Score against your own wishes.
- **Aiming for a perfect circle.** Balance is not the same as equal scores.
- **Too many goals.** Choose one or two areas.
- **One-off.** Repeat regularly to see change.
- **Ignoring context.** A low score can reflect a demanding period, not a failure.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Areas named to fit your life
- [ ] Each scored by feeling, 1 to 10
- [ ] Shape and low areas reviewed
- [ ] Reasons and connections explored
- [ ] One or two focus areas chosen
- [ ] Small actions set and a review date
