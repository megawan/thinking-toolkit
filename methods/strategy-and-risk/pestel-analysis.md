# PESTEL Analysis

> Scan the political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal forces outside your organisation that could shape its future.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Strategy and risk |
| **Time needed** | 1-2 hours for a first pass; research time on top |
| **Best for** | Understanding the external environment before entering a market, setting strategy or planning |

## What it is

PESTEL is a checklist for scanning the macro-environment: factors outside the organisation that it cannot control but must respond to. The six letters are the six categories. The approach traces back to Francis Aguilar's 1967 work on scanning the business environment, and it has several variants, such as PEST and STEEPLE.

| Letter | Factor | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P | Political | Government stability, trade policy, taxation, subsidies, public procurement |
| E | Economic | Growth, inflation, interest rates, exchange rates, unemployment, consumer spending |
| S | Social | Demographics, culture, lifestyles, attitudes, education, health, values |
| T | Technological | New technology, automation, research and development, digital adoption, cybersecurity |
| E | Environmental | Climate, resource scarcity, regulation of emissions, sustainability expectations |
| L | Legal | Employment law, data protection, health and safety, consumer protection, licensing, standards |

## Why it works

Organisations tend to look inwards or at direct competitors. External shifts, such as a new regulation, a change in demographics or a technology, can matter more than anything within the firm. A structured scan prevents blind spots and helps you see risks and opportunities early enough to act.

## When to use

- Entering a new market or country
- Starting a strategy or planning cycle
- Assessing risks to a business or project
- Feeding the Opportunities and Threats of a [SWOT Analysis](swot.md)

## When not to use

- You need to understand the competitive structure; use [Porter's Five Forces](porters-five-forces.md)
- The decision is small and local
- It would be a generic list with no link to a decision

## How to apply

1. **Set the scope:** the organisation, market, region and time horizon (for example 3-5 years).
2. **Brainstorm factors** under each of the six headings. Involve people with different views.
3. **Research** the important ones: use official statistics, industry reports and news.
4. **Rate each factor** for impact (high, medium, low) and likelihood or certainty.
5. **Mark the direction:** opportunity, threat or both.
6. **Prioritise** the few factors that matter most.
7. **Identify trends and links** between factors: for example, a regulation that drives a technology.
8. **Decide on actions:** respond, prepare, monitor or ignore.
9. **Set indicators** to watch and a date to review.

## Prompt questions

| Letter | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Political | Which policies or elections could affect us? Are there trade barriers or subsidies? |
| Economic | How do interest rates and currency movements affect our costs and customers? |
| Social | How are customers' age, habits and values changing? |
| Technological | What technology could make our product obsolete or cheaper to make? |
| Environmental | What climate risks or sustainability rules apply? |
| Legal | What laws and standards must we meet? What is changing? |

## Example: opening a delivery-only restaurant in a city

| Factor | Finding | Direction | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Political | City plans to limit delivery vehicles in the centre | Threat | Medium |
| Economic | Rising food and rent costs; household spending on takeaway steady | Mixed | High |
| Social | More people work from home and order lunch | Opportunity | High |
| Technological | Delivery platforms take 25-30% commission; own ordering app is easier to build | Both | High |
| Environmental | Customers and the city expect less plastic packaging | Threat and opportunity | Medium |
| Legal | Food hygiene licensing for kitchens; labour rules for couriers | Threat | High |

**Actions:** Choose a location outside the restricted zone, build direct ordering to reduce commission, plan recyclable packaging as a selling point, and confirm licensing and courier contracts before signing a lease.

## Pitfalls

- **A long list with no priority.** Rank by impact.
- **Generic entries.** "Technology is changing" does not help; name the technology and its effect.
- **No evidence.** Back factors with data and sources.
- **One-off.** Revisit regularly.
- **Ignoring interactions.** Factors affect each other.
- **No actions.** Finish with decisions and indicators.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Scope and time horizon set
- [ ] All six headings covered
- [ ] Factors backed by evidence
- [ ] Impact and direction rated
- [ ] Top factors prioritised
- [ ] Actions and indicators chosen
