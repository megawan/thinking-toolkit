# SWOT Analysis

> Map what you do well, where you are weak, what the outside offers, and what threatens you, then turn that map into choices.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Analysis and strategy |
| **Time needed** | 45-90 minutes, solo or with a group |
| **Best for** | Evaluating a business, project, product, career move, or plan before committing |

## What it is

SWOT is a structured way to assess a situation along two dimensions: internal versus external, and helpful versus harmful. This gives four quadrants:

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal** (within your control) | **S**trengths | **W**eaknesses |
| **External** (outside your control) | **O**pportunities | **T**hreats |

It became widely used in business planning from the 1960s onward and works equally well for personal and team decisions.

## Why it works

It forces a balanced view. Optimists tend to list only strengths and opportunities, pessimists only risks. The four quadrants make you look at all sides. The distinction between internal and external also clarifies what you can change (your strengths and weaknesses) and what you can only respond to (opportunities and threats).

## When to use

- Starting or reviewing a strategy or plan
- Deciding whether to enter a market, launch a product, or take a role
- Preparing a pitch, proposal, or yearly review
- Aligning a team on where it stands

## When not to use

- You need numbers or forecasts; SWOT is qualitative
- The question is operational and narrow
- As the only input to a major decision

## How to apply

1. **Define the subject and goal.** A SWOT without a goal turns into a vague list. "Should we open a second branch?" is better than "our business".
2. **Gather evidence.** Data, feedback, and observations beat opinions.
3. **List Strengths.** What do you do well? What do you have that others do not?
4. **List Weaknesses.** Where do you fall short? What do others do better? Be honest.
5. **List Opportunities.** What trends, gaps, or changes outside could help?
6. **List Threats.** What outside factors could hurt: competitors, regulation, economy, technology?
7. **Prioritise.** Mark the few items in each quadrant that matter most to the goal.
8. **Combine the quadrants** (see TOWS below) into concrete actions.
9. **Assign owners and review dates.**

## Prompt questions

| Quadrant | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Strengths | What advantages do we have? What do customers praise? What resources are unique? |
| Weaknesses | What do we avoid? Where do we lose? What do customers complain about? |
| Opportunities | What is changing in our favour? What needs are unmet? What could a partner bring? |
| Threats | Who is growing against us? What rules or costs are changing? What are we dependent on? |

## From list to action: TOWS

A SWOT list is only the start. Pair the quadrants:

| Pairing | Strategy |
| --- | --- |
| Strength + Opportunity | Use strengths to capture opportunities |
| Strength + Threat | Use strengths to reduce or avoid threats |
| Weakness + Opportunity | Fix weaknesses so you can take opportunities |
| Weakness + Threat | Reduce exposure; avoid the worst case |

## Example

**Subject:** A small bakery considering online delivery.

| | Helpful | Harmful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Internal** | **Strengths:** loyal local customers; strong reputation for sourdough; owner-baker control of quality | **Weaknesses:** no delivery vehicle; small kitchen capacity; no website |
| **External** | **Opportunities:** growing demand for local food delivery; nearby offices with no bakery; subscription boxes | **Threats:** supermarket chains offering similar bread; rising flour prices; delivery apps taking 25-30% |

**Actions from TOWS:**
- S+O: Offer a weekly sourdough subscription to nearby offices (use reputation and loyal base)
- W+O: Start with pickup-point orders to avoid buying a vehicle
- S+T: Position on quality, not price, against supermarkets
- W+T: Set a daily order cap to protect kitchen capacity and avoid delivery app fees

## Using it for personal decisions

Subject: "Should I change career into data analysis?" Strengths: strong maths, current employer's data exposure. Weaknesses: no formal training. Opportunities: growing demand. Threats: entry-level competition. Action: take a short course while in the current role, build two portfolio projects, then apply.

## Pitfalls

- **Vague items.** "Good team" is weak; "team of six with ten years each in logistics" is useful.
- **Mixing internal and external.** Check each item against the quadrant definition.
- **Wishful strengths.** Test strengths against how customers or competitors see you.
- **Long lists with no priority.** Cut to the few that matter.
- **Stopping at the list.** Without actions, SWOT changes nothing.
- **Treating it as permanent.** Update it when the situation changes.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Subject and goal defined
- [ ] Items backed by evidence
- [ ] Internal and external correctly separated
- [ ] Top items prioritised
- [ ] Quadrants combined into actions
- [ ] Owners and review date set
