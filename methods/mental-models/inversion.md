# Inversion

> Instead of asking how to succeed, ask how to fail, then avoid exactly that.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Category** | Mental models |
| **Time needed** | 10-30 minutes, solo or in a group |
| **Best for** | Planning, risk spotting, and problems where the direct approach has not worked |

## What it is

Inversion means thinking about a problem backwards. The mathematician Carl Jacobi is known for the advice "invert, always invert". Charlie Munger made it a core part of his approach to decisions: "Tell me where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there."

There are two forms:

- **Invert the goal.** Rather than "how can I be happy?", ask "what would make me miserable?" and avoid those things.
- **Invert the assumptions.** Take a belief and ask what would be true if the opposite held.

## Why it works

People are better at spotting errors than at designing perfection. Many fields advance more by avoiding obvious mistakes than by finding brilliant moves. Inversion turns a vague goal into a concrete list of things to avoid, and often exposes risks that a forward plan would hide.

## When to use

- A forward plan feels vague or stuck
- You want to find risks before they appear
- You are setting goals and want to remove the obstacles to them
- A group is too optimistic about a plan

## When not to use

- Avoiding failure is not enough to produce success; use it alongside a positive plan
- The problem has no clear opposite
- You need creative new options, where [Lateral Thinking](../ideation/lateral-thinking.md) fits better

## How to apply

1. **State the goal** as a positive outcome.
2. **Invert it.** Restate it as the opposite: "How could we guarantee that this fails?"
3. **List the ways** to reach the opposite. Be specific, honest, and exhaustive. Be ruthless about what you would be tempted to do.
4. **Flip each item** into a rule or action: "avoid X" or "do the opposite of X".
5. **Check what is already happening.** Are you doing any of the failure items now?
6. **Prioritise.** Remove or prevent the most damaging and most likely ones first.
7. **Combine with a forward plan.** Add the positive steps that inversion alone does not give you.

## Example 1: A product launch

**Goal:** A successful launch.

**Inverted:** How would we guarantee a failed launch?

- Launch without talking to any customers
- Set the date by wish, not by capacity
- Skip testing to hit the date
- Give support no training
- Change the scope every week

**Rules from the inversion:**

- Interview customers before locking scope
- Set the date from an estimate with buffer
- Freeze scope two weeks before the date
- Train support before release
- Run a staged rollout with a test group

**Check:** The team realises scope has been changing weekly, which is already on the failure list. They set a freeze date.

## Example 2: Personal health

**Goal:** Stay healthy for decades.

**Inverted:** What would reliably ruin my health? Sleeping five hours, smoking, never moving, skipping check-ups, constant stress.

**Result:** Fix sleep and movement first; book a check-up. Avoiding these gives more benefit than searching for a perfect diet.

## Example 3: Inverting an assumption

**Assumption:** "Customers want more features."

**Inverse:** What if customers want fewer? That leads to checking usage data, finding that most features are never used, and testing a simpler version.

## Tips

- Pair it with [Pre-mortem](../strategy-and-risk/pre-mortem.md) for team projects
- Write failure causes out in plain words; avoid softening them
- Ask "what are we doing now that is on this list?"
- Revisit the list when the plan changes

## Pitfalls

- **Stopping at avoiding.** Not failing is not the same as succeeding; add positive actions.
- **Vague failure lists.** "Bad communication" is hard to act on; "no weekly status update" is not.
- **Pessimism.** The aim is to find fixable errors, not to talk yourself out of acting.
- **Ignoring what you already do.** The most useful step is to compare the list with current behaviour.
- **Taking the inverse literally.** Some opposites are not meaningful; use the form that helps.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Goal stated
- [ ] Inverse stated clearly
- [ ] Failure causes listed specifically
- [ ] Each flipped into a rule or action
- [ ] Current behaviour checked against the list
- [ ] Positive steps added
