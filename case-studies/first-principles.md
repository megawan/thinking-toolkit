# Case study: The cost of custom shelving

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [First Principles](../methods/mental-models/first-principles.md)

## Context

A small furniture maker sold custom shelves at a price customers found high, and competitors charged roughly the same.

## What was done

- Instead of matching competitors, the maker broke the price into materials, labour, delivery and retail margin
- Raw materials were a small part of the price; most of the rest was delivering a bulky finished item and a store margin
- Challenged assumptions: it must be sold assembled, and it must be sold through a store

## Outcome

The maker tested flat-pack, cut-to-size panels sold directly online. Shipping volume fell, the store margin disappeared, and the price dropped substantially.

## Lessons

- The facts (what it is made of) differed from the habits (how it is sold)
- Rebuilding from facts opened an option competitors had not considered
- A small test came before changing the whole line
