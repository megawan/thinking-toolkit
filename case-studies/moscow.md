# Case study: First release of a booking app

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [MoSCoW](../methods/prioritisation/moscow.md)

## Context

A small team had 100 person-days before a fixed launch date and a wish list of more than 130 days. Every stakeholder called their item essential.

## What was done

- The team agreed on a test for Must: the release is pointless or unusable without it
- Search and booking, payment and confirmation email were Musts (55 days, 55 percent of capacity)
- Cancel and reschedule and SMS reminders were Shoulds; reviews and dark mode were Coulds; loyalty points were Won't this time
- They kept the remaining 45 days of capacity as a buffer for Shoulds and Coulds

## Outcome

The team shipped on time with the Musts and cancellation, dropped dark mode when an estimate grew, and scheduled loyalty points for the next release.

## Lessons

- The Won't group let people feel heard without growing scope
- Challenging each Must cut the list sharply
- The capacity check turned a wish list into a plan
