# Case study: A meeting about a reporting tool

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Logical Fallacies](../methods/critical-thinking/logical-fallacies.md)

## Context

A team was choosing between its old reporting tool and a newer one from a small vendor. One member argued against the change in a way that persuaded several colleagues.

## What was done

- The argument was: 'The vendor is a start-up with only ten staff, and everyone knows start-ups fail'
- A colleague restated it, then pointed out the hasty generalisation (start-ups in general versus this vendor) and the red herring (vendor size is not the same as fit for the team's needs)
- She named what was missing: the old tool's cost, the new tool's features and the vendor's finances
- She acknowledged that vendor risk is a fair concern, and proposed to check the vendor's finances and the team's exit options

## Outcome

The team compared both tools on the features it needed and asked the vendor for references and financial information. The decision rested on evidence, not on the first, confident remark.

## Lessons

- She named the flaw in plain words and kept the valid concern
- She did not claim the conclusion was false because the argument was flawed
- She asked for what was missing
