# Case study: Explaining compound interest

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Feynman Technique](../methods/reflection-and-learning/feynman-technique.md)

## Context

A new employee in a finance team could recite the definition of compound interest but was asked by a customer to explain why it grows faster than simple interest, and could not.

## What was done

- First attempt: the textbook definition, with 'principal' left undefined and no reason given for the faster growth
- Gaps: no example, no explanation of why the growth accelerates
- Second attempt: a bank pays 10 percent on 100, so after a year there is 110; in year two the interest is 11, not 10, because interest itself earns interest; like a snowball
- A colleague asked what happens after 20 years, and the new employee worked out about 673 against 300 for simple interest

## Outcome

The employee added the numbers to a one-page note and used the explanation with the next customer.

## Lessons

- The gap showed where to study: why, not just what
- Concrete numbers made the idea easy to repeat
- A real listener found a question the written version had missed
