# Case study: A hiring decision

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Decision Journal](../methods/reflection-and-learning/decision-journal.md)

## Context

A team lead had to fill a role quickly and chose between a strongly technical candidate with little customer experience and one with the opposite profile.

## What was done

- She recorded her reasons (the technical gap was biggest and customer skills can be taught), her assumption (the candidate would learn within six months), a 65 percent expectation of good customer performance by September, and the fact she was under time pressure
- In September the new hire was excellent technically, but customers found communication difficult and there were two complaints
- She concluded that 65 percent was too high with no evidence of customer skill, and that time pressure had led her to discount the other candidate's strengths

## Outcome

She added a role-play for customer skills in hiring for customer-facing roles and a second reviewer for decisions made under time pressure.

## Lessons

- The written record showed the reasoning at the time and could not be rewritten
- Separating skill from luck pointed to a process fix
- The probability made it possible to see she was overconfident
