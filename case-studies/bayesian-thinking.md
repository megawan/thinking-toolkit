# Case study: Reading a positive medical test

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Bayesian Thinking](../methods/mental-models/bayesian-thinking.md)

## Context

A health-literacy workshop used a standard example: 1 percent of people have a condition, the test is positive for 90 percent of those who have it and falsely positive for 9 percent of those who do not.

## What was done

- Participants worked with 1,000 people: 10 have the condition and 9 test positive; 990 do not, and about 89 test positive
- About 98 people test positive, and only 9 of them have the condition
- The chance that a positive person has the condition is about 9 percent, not 90

## Outcome

Most participants first guessed 80 to 90 percent. After the exercise they could explain why the base rate matters, and why follow-up tests are used.

## Lessons

- Natural frequencies made the answer easy to see
- The test was useful (1 to 9 percent) but not conclusive
- The same pattern applies to fraud alerts and security warnings
