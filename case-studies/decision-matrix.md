# Case study: Choosing a project tool for a ten-person team

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Decision Matrix](../methods/decision-making/decision-matrix.md)

## Context

A growing team used spreadsheets and chat to track work, and three people each preferred a different tool. Discussion kept restarting.

## What was done

- The team agreed first on five criteria: ease of use, cost, integrations, reporting and support
- They weighted ease of use and cost highest
- Each person scored the three tools alone, then scores were compared
- Differences of two or more points were discussed

## Outcome

One tool led by a few points, and raising the cost weight did not change the ranking. The team confirmed the price fit the budget and adopted it with a one-month review.

## Lessons

- Agreeing the weights first removed most of the argument
- Scoring alone first avoided anchoring on the loudest person
- Small score gaps were treated as a tie, so a review date mattered
