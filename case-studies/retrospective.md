# Case study: A software team after a sprint

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Retrospective](../methods/reflection-and-learning/retrospective.md)

## Context

A product team missed its sprint goal twice in a row, and members privately blamed 'too many meetings' and 'changing priorities'.

## What was done

- They ran a Start / Stop / Continue retrospective after stating that everyone had done the best they could with what they knew
- Start: a short daily check on blocked tasks. Stop: adding work in the middle of the sprint and long status meetings. Continue: customer demos and a shared on-call rota
- The biggest theme was unplanned work in mid-sprint
- Two actions: new requests wait for the next sprint unless the product owner swaps something out, and the status meeting becomes a ten-minute standing check

## Outcome

At the next retrospective the team opened with those actions. Mid-sprint additions fell, the status meeting was shorter, and the sprint goal was met.

## Lessons

- Silent writing let quieter members raise the unplanned-work issue
- Two small actions were completed; a long list would not have been
- Checking last time's actions first kept the habit alive
