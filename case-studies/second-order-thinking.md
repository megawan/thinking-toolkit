# Case study: A bonus for closing support tickets

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Second-order Thinking](../methods/mental-models/second-order-thinking.md)

## Context

A support manager wanted tickets closed faster and proposed a bonus for every ticket closed.

## What was done

- First-order effect: tickets would close faster
- Second-order: agents might close tickets before the problem was solved, customers would reopen them, and easy tickets would be picked first
- Third-order: satisfaction would fall, hard issues would pile up and the best agents, who handle the hard ones, would leave
- The manager redesigned the bonus around first-contact resolution and satisfaction, with reopened tickets counted against it

## Outcome

The redesigned bonus kept speed in view without rewarding closing for its own sake. The team tracked reopen rate as an early warning.

## Lessons

- The question 'what behaviour does this reward?' found the flaw
- A reopen-rate indicator turned a prediction into something watchable
- The adjustment was cheap compared with fixing the damage afterwards
