# Case study: A four-day week proposal

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Steel-manning](../methods/critical-thinking/steel-manning.md)

## Context

A manager opposed a proposed four-day week because of customer coverage, and had so far argued against 'people working less for the same pay'.

## What was done

- She restated the supporters' case: focused time and rest raise output and cut burnout, coverage can be kept with rotating cover, and a trial could measure results
- She added their evidence on turnover and hiring costs, and asked the proposer whether she had got it right
- The proposer added that the pilot would be reversible

## Outcome

Instead of a flat refusal, the manager proposed a three-month trial with defined coverage and success measures. The team agreed, and the proposer felt the concern was taken seriously.

## Lessons

- The straw man ('work less for the same pay') hid the actual argument
- Agreement on the pilot came from answering the real concern
- The restatement was checked with the proposer
