# Case study: Bezos and the choice to start Amazon

> Documented example. It is based on the source listed below, and the details are limited to what that source reports. The source is a secondhand retelling, so treat it with care.

Method: [Regret Minimization](../methods/decision-making/regret-minimization.md)

## Context

Jeff Bezos was working at D. E. Shaw & Co., a quantitative hedge fund in New York, in a secure and well-paid position, when he considered leaving to start an online bookstore in Seattle. A 2016 blog post by Ben Carlson, quoting the book *Algorithms to Live By* by Brian Christian and Tom Griffiths, tells the story. It says his boss advised him to think about the decision carefully.

## What was done

According to that retelling, Bezos imagined himself at age 80, looking back, and wanted to have as few regrets as possible. He called this a "regret minimization framework". He judged that:

- He would not regret trying the Internet venture, and failing would not bother him
- What might haunt him was never having tried

The decision then became, in his words as quoted, "incredibly easy".

## Outcome

He left to start the company that became Amazon. The source describes the choice as a "big leap" and gives no further results; the success of the company is outside what the source discusses.

## Lessons

- Zooming out to the end of a life or career moves attention from the fear of the moment to what matters over time
- He compared two kinds of regret (trying and failing, or never trying) and found the second stronger
- The framework made a risky choice feel clear without removing the risk: the downside, failure, was one he judged he could accept
- The choice was personal and about values; the method does not tell anyone which way to decide

## Caveats

- The post gives no date for the decision and cites no primary source for Bezos's words, such as an interview or video; it relies on the book's retelling. The year, widely reported as 1994, is not in the source read for this entry.
- The post reports only that his boss told him to think carefully, not what was said in the conversation
- The case shows the framework in use but not whether the framework, rather than other factors, caused the outcome
- The source is a blog post and was not checked against the book
