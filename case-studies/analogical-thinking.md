# Case study: Queues at a clinic reception

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Analogical Thinking](../methods/ideation/analogical-thinking.md)

## Context

A community clinic had a crowded reception desk in the morning. Patients stood in a line, many unwell, and waiting felt stressful.

## What was done

- The team restated the problem in general terms: many people need a small piece of service from one point, and urgency varies
- They looked at a deli counter (numbered tickets), a restaurant pager (alert when ready), airline online check-in (do the form before arrival) and emergency triage (sort by urgency)
- They mapped each back to the clinic and noted where it breaks, for example urgent cases cannot wait for a ticket

## Outcome

The clinic adopted pre-visit forms, numbered tickets with a seating area and text alerts, with a short sorting step at the door for urgent cases. The desk line became much shorter.

## Lessons

- Abstracting the problem made far-away fields relevant
- The 'where it breaks' step added the urgent-case exception
- Several analogies were combined into one solution
