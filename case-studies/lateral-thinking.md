# Case study: An internal newsletter nobody reads

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Lateral Thinking](../methods/ideation/lateral-thinking.md)

## Context

A 200-person company sent a weekly newsletter with an open rate under 20 percent. Shortening it and changing the subject lines made little difference.

## What was done

- Challenge assumptions: it must be email, weekly, and sent to everyone
- Reversal: how would we make sure nobody reads it? Make it long, general and late
- Random word 'lighthouse': short, visible, and only for warnings that matter
- Provocation: 'the newsletter has one sentence'

## Outcome

They moved to a short alert only when something affects people's work, plus a monthly page on the lobby screen. Open rates on alerts rose well above the old newsletter's, and a monthly page replaced the weekly mail.

## Lessons

- The useful ideas came from reversal and the random word, not from improving the old format
- Odd ideas gave a direction, not the final design
- A small test (one month) settled it
