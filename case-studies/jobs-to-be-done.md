# Case study: A meal-kit service finds its real jobs

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Jobs To Be Done](../methods/design-and-product/jobs-to-be-done.md)

## Context

A meal-kit start-up had steady sign-ups but high cancellation, and its marketing focused on 'gourmet recipes'. The team did not know why customers joined or left.

## What was done

- They interviewed 12 recent customers about the story of their first order
- Three jobs emerged: a proper weekday meal without planning, feeling like a good parent who feeds the family well, and trying new cooking with low risk
- The real alternatives were takeaway, ready meals and a pasta dish, not other meal kits
- Customers who left had been hired for the first job, but spent 50 minutes cooking

## Outcome

The team changed the headline to 'dinner in 30 minutes, no planning', added a 30-minutes-or-less filter, and added a gentle first-recipe guide for beginners. Cancellations fell in the following months.

## Lessons

- The job explained both why people joined and why they left
- Competitors were takeaway and ready meals
- Interviews about what happened gave more than asking what customers wanted
