# Case study: A software project estimate

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Cognitive Biases](../methods/critical-thinking/cognitive-biases.md)

## Context

A team estimated a software project at six months. The sponsor had hoped for six, and the team lead spoke first in the estimation meeting.

## What was done

- The team checked for biases and found four likely ones: the planning fallacy (their last three projects ran 40 to 60 percent over), anchoring on the sponsor's hope, overconfidence in single-point estimates, and groupthink from the lead speaking first
- They re-estimated independently and bottom up, gave each task a range, and wrote down estimates before discussing them

## Outcome

The revised plan said eight months with a range of seven to ten. The sponsor chose a smaller first release instead.

## Lessons

- Each bias had a concrete countermeasure
- Writing estimates before discussion changed the process, not only intentions
- The history of past projects was the best check on optimism
