# Case study: Buying a 3D printer for a small workshop

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Cost-Benefit Analysis](../methods/decision-making/cost-benefit-analysis.md)

## Context

A small workshop outsourced prototype prints for about $2,000 a year and considered buying a $4,000 printer.

## What was done

- The owner listed the purchase price, $600 a year for materials and maintenance, and $2,000 a year of savings over five years
- At an 8 percent discount rate, present value of benefits was about $7,985 and of costs about $6,396, an NPV of roughly $1,590
- Sensitivity showed that if savings fell to $1,500 a year, NPV turned negative; break-even was about $1,600 a year

## Outcome

Because the result depended on volume, the owner counted how many prints were outsourced in the last year before buying, and confirmed it was above the break-even level.

## Lessons

- Break-even turned a marginal NPV into a concrete check
- Recurring costs mattered as much as the price
- The same rate and horizon would be needed to compare it with other options
