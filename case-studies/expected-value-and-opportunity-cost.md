# Case study: Launching a product or taking a consulting contract

> Illustrative scenario. It is a composite written to show the method in use, not a report about a real organisation.

Method: [Expected Value and Opportunity Cost](../methods/decision-making/expected-value-and-opportunity-cost.md)

## Context

A freelance developer had three months free. She could launch her own product or take a consulting contract for a certain $45,000.

## What was done

- She estimated the launch: 30 percent big success ($200,000), 50 percent modest ($20,000) and 20 percent failure (-$60,000), giving an EV of $58,000
- The consulting contract was the next-best option, so its $45,000 was the opportunity cost of the launch
- Net gain of launching was $13,000, but with a one-in-five chance of losing $60,000
- When she lowered the big-success chance to 20 percent, with 55 percent modest and 25 percent failure, EV fell to $36,000, below the contract

## Outcome

Because the choice depended on one estimate, she ran a small demand test first. It supported the optimistic case and she launched with a spending cap.

## Lessons

- The opportunity cost made the real comparison clear
- Sensitivity showed which estimate mattered
- Checking the worst case led to a spending cap
